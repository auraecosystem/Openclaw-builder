# Qullamaggie Episodic Pivot (EP)

A catalyst-driven long setup. A stock gaps on genuinely surprising news, institutions reprice it quickly, and that day can start a multi-week or multi-month trend.

## What Qualifies As A Catalyst

- Political or regulatory change
- FDA or biotech approval
- Major contracts or partnerships
- Earnings surprise with upward guidance revision
- Sector-wide catalyst inside a hot group

> "News that catches the market off guard and forces a revaluation of the stock."

For earnings EPs, the ideal case is triple-digit year-over-year earnings and sales growth, a clear analyst beat, and materially raised guidance.

## Exact Setup Definition

This is a surprise revaluation setup. The stock should be repriced because the market did not already discount the news.

## Scanning For Candidates

Use the EP scan and then manually filter for true surprise:

- One-day move greater than 7.5%
- Gap of at least 10% from the prior close
- Dollar volume at least $100M on the gap day
- Exceptional opening volume, ideally a full ADV trading in the first 15-20 minutes
- Best candidate was quiet or sideways for roughly 3-6 months before the gap

Exclude immediately:

- Stocks that already had a large 3-6 month run into the gap
- “Second EP” situations that recently had another major EP
- Vague or weak catalysts
- Illiquid names that cannot handle disciplined size
- Sympathy moves without their own true revaluation catalyst

## Setup Criteria

- Gap of at least 10% from the prior close on catalyst day
- EP scan threshold is one-day move above 7.5% with at least $100M dollar volume on the gap day
- Stock should not already have run hard in the prior 3-6 months
- Ideal precondition is a sideways base lasting 3-6 months before the gap

> "The failure rate is higher and the move probably won't be as big" if the stock already had a recent prior EP.

## Volume Requirement

The main confirmation is institutional volume at the open.

> "Massive volume near the open. Ideally the stock should trade the average daily volume the first 15-20 minutes or even quicker."

Trading a full day of average volume in the first 15-20 minutes is the key confirmation filter.

## What A Good Setup Looks Like

- The news is truly surprising and forces a new valuation
- The stock was neglected before the gap
- The opening volume is overwhelming and obvious
- Price holds the gap and the opening range rather than instantly fading
- The sector theme, if any, is strong enough to support follow-through
- The stock has room for institutions to continue repricing it over days or weeks

## What A Weak Setup Looks Like

- The story sounds exciting, but the gap is small or the volume is unimpressive
- The stock already rallied materially before the supposed “surprise”
- The move is more hype than fundamental revaluation
- The name is too thin to manage properly
- Price cannot hold above the opening range for long

## Trap Setups And Red Herrings

- **Already-ran trap:** stock gaps on good news, but the market largely expected it because the stock already advanced for months
- **Sympathy-gap trap:** related names move, but the stock itself has no real fresh catalyst
- **Low-volume EP trap:** headline looks great, but there is no true institutional volume signature
- **Second-EP trap:** traders treat every follow-on gap as the same quality as the original surprise gap
- **Open-chase trap:** buying before ORH confirmation because the gap “looks too good”

## Entry Checklist

1. The catalyst is truly surprising and forces repricing.
2. The stock did not already run hard in the prior 3-6 months.
3. The gap is at least 10%.
4. Dollar volume on the gap day is at least $100M.
5. Opening volume is exceptional, ideally a full ADV trading in 15-20 minutes.
6. The entry trigger occurs in the first 3-10 minutes or on a valid later continuation high.

## Entry Timing

- Best entries are typically 3-10 minutes after the open
- Primary trigger is the 1-minute ORH
- Can add on 5-minute highs for confirmation and size
- If the 1-minute trigger is missed, can still use 5-minute or 60-minute highs
- Can continue adding through the day if the action remains strong

## Stop Loss

- Stop is the low of the gap day
- If the trade is initiated the next day, the stop still references the original gap-day low
- Maximum width is normally 1x ADR, occasionally 1.5x ADR

**Sizing rule**

- Position size is still determined from the stop distance versus the chosen account risk
- If the gap-day low is too wide to size correctly, skip or reduce size rather than improvising

## Hold Duration And Context

- EPs can become multi-week to multi-month trends
- Trail with the 10-day or 20-day MA
- EPs are more resilient than ordinary breakouts in mixed or weak market conditions
- He will still trade EPs in bear markets, but with smaller size and lower expectations

> "Takes 4-8 earnings seasons (1-2 years) to get good at this setup."

This is described as the best raw risk/reward setup in the playbook because the stop is tight relative to the upside when the revaluation is real.

## No-Trade Conditions

- The catalyst is vague or already well anticipated
- The stock already had a major run before the supposed EP
- Opening volume is not extraordinary
- The stock fails to hold its gap structure quickly
- The stop at the gap-day low creates unacceptable risk

## Disciplined Execution Sequence

1. Verify catalyst and prior-neglect condition.
2. Verify the gap and volume thresholds.
3. Wait for the 1-minute ORH or later continuation high.
4. Anchor the stop to the gap-day low immediately.
5. Size from that stop, not from a desired P&L outcome.
6. Hold for the larger trend only if the stock continues to respect the 10-day / 20-day management logic.
