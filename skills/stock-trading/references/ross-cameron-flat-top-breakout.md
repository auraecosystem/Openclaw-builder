# Ross Cameron Flat Top Breakout

Variation of the continuation setup where the stock keeps pressing the same resistance level until it finally breaks.

## Pattern Structure

- Strong momentum move into resistance
- Repeated tests of a horizontal ceiling
- Pullbacks stay shallow
- Sellers fail to force a deeper flush

The key feature is the obvious flat top. Everyone can see the same level.

## Scanning For Candidates

This setup is usually found from the HOD momentum scanner in a stock that already passed the broader small-cap momentum filters.

Look for:

- A leading stock with a real catalyst
- Price repeatedly pressing one clear ceiling
- Shallow pullbacks between tests
- Volume compression into the level and expansion on the break
- Often a psychologically important level such as HOD, half-dollar, or whole-dollar resistance

Exclude immediately:

- Stocks with ambiguous resistance
- Names that are not the leading mover
- Repeated failed breaks that are getting weaker
- Setups where the stop under the base is too wide

## Entry

- Buy the break above the flat ceiling / repeated resistance high
- The more obvious and better-defined the ceiling, the cleaner the trigger

## Valid Pattern Requirements

- The stock is already one of the leading names of the day
- Resistance is clearly horizontal and repeatedly tested
- Pullbacks remain shallow and constructive
- Volume contracts during the base and expands on the break

## What A Good Setup Looks Like

- Ceiling is obvious to everyone
- Each dip is shallow and controlled
- Buyers keep reloading near the same area
- The breakout level is close enough to allow tight risk
- The stock still has urgency and momentum behind it

## What A Weak Setup Looks Like

- The “flat top” is not actually flat
- Pullbacks get deeper after each test
- Buyers are no longer able to push back near highs
- Break attempts get smaller and less energetic
- The stock is technically still up but already losing leadership

## Trap Setups And Red Herrings

- **Double-top trap:** two equal highs with heavy rejection are not automatically a proper flat-top breakout
- **First-spike trap:** the stock hits resistance once and traders call it a flat top before any real consolidation forms
- **Low-volume break trap:** price trades above the ceiling without real momentum behind it
- **Too-many-tests trap:** after repeated failures, the level can become a trap instead of a springboard

## Stop Loss

- Stop generally goes below the flat base or the most recent pullback low

## Management Rules

- Pre-trade reward-to-risk must still be at least 2:1
- Tight patterns are preferred because they allow size without excessive risk
- If price breaks the ceiling and instantly fails, exit quickly

## What Makes It Attractive

- The trigger is simple and visible
- Tight risk is often possible
- It works well on stocks that are already the leading momentum name of the day

## Failure Signs

- Each test of resistance gets weaker
- Pullbacks become deeper
- Heavy sellers appear on the ask / Level 2
- Breakout goes through the level but immediately fails

## No-Trade Conditions

- The ceiling is not actually flat or obvious
- The stock keeps rejecting harder each time it tests the level
- The stop under the base is too wide to size correctly
