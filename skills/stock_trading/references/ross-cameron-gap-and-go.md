# Ross Cameron Gap and Go

Primary opening setup. A stock gaps pre-market on a real catalyst, holds up into the open, and then extends higher as momentum traders pile in.

## Core Criteria

1. Gap is meaningful, usually more than 4%
2. Catalyst is real and verifiable
3. The stock passes the broader small-cap momentum filters
4. Pre-market price action is constructive, not fading hard into the open
5. Relative volume should be extreme, often 5x or more
6. Price should generally be within the small-cap sweet spot, often $1-$20
7. Float should ideally be under 10M, especially under 5M for the most explosive names

## Scanning For Candidates

Use the pre-market gap scanner first, then rank by quality:

- Gap greater than 4%, with 10%+ preferred
- Relative volume around 5x or more
- Price generally in the $1-$20 range
- Float ideally under 10M, especially under 5M
- Real catalyst, manually verified
- Stronger names often show useful pre-market volume, with A-quality gappers often above roughly 150,000 shares pre-market

Exclude immediately:

- Gaps under 4%
- No real catalyst
- Float too large to move well
- Weak pre-market volume
- Spread too wide to manage properly
- Premarket fade that already suggests the gap is failing

## Key Levels

- Pre-market high
- Pre-market low
- First 1-minute candle high and low
- Any obvious micro flag under the pre-market high

## Entry Methods

**1-minute opening range breakout**

- Buy the break of the high of the first 1-minute candle after 9:30
- Stop is usually the low of that same candle

**Pre-market high break**

- Buy as price breaks the pre-market high
- Stop can reference the nearest intraday support or, more loosely, the pre-market low

**Position construction**

- He may take a starter first and then add on confirmation
- If the stop distance makes position size too large or too small relative to the plan, resize or skip

## What A Good Setup Looks Like

- The stock is the leading pre-market gapper or one of the clear leaders
- The gap is tied to a real catalyst
- Pre-market price holds near the top of the gap
- Pre-market high is obvious and actionable
- Float is low enough that expansion can happen quickly
- The first opening push confirms instead of stalling immediately

## What A Weak Setup Looks Like

- The stock is gapping, but fading through most of pre-market
- Volume looks mediocre relative to the size of the move
- The catalyst is soft, stale, or unclear
- Float is too large to expect clean small-cap momentum
- The stock is not really the leading name of the morning

## Trap Setups And Red Herrings

- **Sub-4% gap trap:** a stock is green pre-market, but the gap is too small and more likely to fill
- **No-news gap trap:** a gapper appears active but lacks a clean reason for the move
- **Reverse-split data trap:** scanner-reported float can be misleading after reverse splits; verify it
- **Opening-chase trap:** price rips through the level and the trader chases far above the trigger
- **Late-gap trap:** trying to force Gap and Go logic after the first 30 minutes have already passed

## Failed Gap Characteristics

- Selling off throughout pre-market
- Weak volume relative to the size of the gap
- No real catalyst
- Immediate rejection of the opening push

In practice, the best versions of this setup tend to hold near the top of the gap rather than bleed lower before the bell.

## Timing

- Best during the first 30 minutes after the open
- Usually the most time-sensitive Ross Cameron setup

## Management Rules

- Pre-trade target must still be at least 2:1 reward to risk
- First profit target often takes part of the position off
- After the first partial, stop on the remainder can move to breakeven
- If the breakout stalls immediately, bail out fast rather than letting an opening setup turn into a hold-and-hope trade

## No-Trade Conditions

- The stock gaps but fades hard through pre-market
- The catalyst is unclear or weak
- The spread is too wide
- Reward-to-risk is under 2:1
- The setup appears after the initial opening window and has already lost urgency
