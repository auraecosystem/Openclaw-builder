## Core Lens

- Buy strong stocks in good markets with momentum. The pattern is not enough by itself.
- A stock should have a reason to move: hot sector, strong story, clean earnings, strong growth, or a catalyst the market cares about.
- Momentum, tightness, and relative strength are the key variables. Loose charts and average names do not get better because they look cheap.
- The basic long-side rhythm is staircase behavior: strong move, pause or pullback, then another move higher.
- Buy tight and then sit tight. The edge comes from small predefined losses and occasional outsized winners, not from high win rate.
- Better to own a mediocre stock in the hottest sector than a great stock in a dead sector.
- Do not confuse knowledge with edge. The goal is to know enough to trade well without overthinking yourself out of the right names.

## Market Regime Rules

- The best breakout environment is when the market is coming out of a correction and fresh setups are starting to work again.
- The primary long-side green light is the index 10-day above the 20-day with both rising. If the 10-day is below the 20-day, breakout odds are materially worse.
- Extended tape changes the rules. After a 6-7 week run, fresh breakouts are more likely to be laggards or late-stage setups.
- When indexes are extended but many names are still setting up, breakouts can continue to work. When quality setups start drying up, get more defensive.
- Mixed tape matters even if the index is not broken. If many names stall while only a few continue, speculation money is leaving and size should come down.
- Nothing holds up well in a broad 10% market drawdown. Correlation rises and even leaders lose support.
- Distinguish aggressive periods from slow periods. There are weeks when the right trade is to do less or nothing.

## Stock And Sector Selection

- Start with the top relative-strength names and the hottest sectors, not with random symbols that happen to print a pattern.
- When a sector starts moving, immediately look for the second-order and third-order names in that group. Big-cap leaders often pull smaller names higher later.
- Small caps usually need a catalyst because there is less natural institutional sponsorship.
- Sub-dollar names do not belong in this framework and are often structurally broken or near-bankruptcy situations.
- Triple-digit year-over-year EPS and revenue growth are major positives, especially when forward estimates are also strong.
- High current growth with falling forward estimates is weaker than high growth with durable future demand.
- Avoid random mediocre-growth names with no catalyst. Too much randomness destroys the edge.
- Examples like SPACs or blockchain names are period-specific expressions of the same rule: trade what is hot now, not what used to be hot.

## Continuation Breakout Rules

- The stock should have already made a real prior move. A breakout without prior momentum is usually just range noise.
- After the impulse, the stock should pull back or go sideways while finding support on the 10-day, 20-day, or sometimes 50-day moving average.
- Higher lows before the breakout materially improve the setup. So do undercut-and-reclaim candles through a key moving average.
- Tightness is mandatory. A good setup compresses into a multi-day or multi-week range so the breakout level and stop are both clear.
- The best setups show the 10-day and 20-day rising hard, often roughly at a 45-degree angle, while the 20-day catches up beneath price.
- Weekly flags that go sideways for roughly 3-4 months can be especially powerful. Longer, cleaner formations are usually better than rushed breakouts.
- If a chart is still wide, loose, or swinging violently, there is no edge. At most, that is a starter-only candidate until the 60-minute chart tightens.

## Breakout Quality Filters

- Require clean prior price action. Stocks tend to keep behaving like themselves, so a history of choppy, sloppy action is a real warning sign.
- Favor names that repeatedly respect the same moving average. If a stock has repeatedly held the 10-day or 20-day, a new setup there carries more weight.
- An undercut of the 10-day that wicks through and closes back above it is often very bullish price action when the broader setup is still intact.
- Avoid names that retraced too far or too long, especially below the 50-day. Once the stock has truly lost momentum, the continuation edge is mostly gone.
- Avoid late-stage breakouts after multiple prior flag launches or a massive extended run. Stocks do not go up forever.
- Breakouts should occur on clearly above-normal daily volume. On faster names, an hourly volume spike is a useful confirmation.
- A gap up in an already extended market that immediately sells off is usually a bad sign, not hidden strength.
- When drawing ranges and flags, focus on the area with the most touches and the clearest real structure. Ignore a few noisy outlier candles if they do not change the underlying trend.

## Continuation Breakout 5-Star Checklist

This is an inferred scoring checklist, not an official numeric Qullamaggie rubric.

Use `100` points total:

- `85+`: `5-star`
- `70-84`: good but not elite
- below `70`: not worth treating as a top continuation-breakout candidate

If any of the top four items fail, the setup is not `5-star` no matter what the raw total says.

1. `20 points` - Real prior momentum move in a true leader.
   The stock should already have made a meaningful move in the prior `1-3 months` and rank among the market's real leaders, not just be a random base in a weak name.
2. `18 points` - Tightness at the pivot.
   The base should get tighter and tighter into the breakout. Loose, wide, or unstable bases immediately fall out of `5-star` territory.
3. `15 points` - Higher lows into the breakout.
   Price should compress upward, not sag sideways. The best setups repeatedly show higher lows or a subtle undercut-and-reclaim before the move.
4. `14 points` - Support on rising short moving averages.
   The best names hold the `10-day`, very strong names can surf the `20-day`, and still-valid but slightly weaker names may hold the `50-day`.
5. `10 points` - Clean trend quality before the base.
   The prior uptrend should be smooth and orderly above key moving averages.
6. `9 points` - Volume dry-up in the base, then expansion on breakout.
   Supply should visibly disappear into the pivot and then return on the breakout day.
7. `7 points` - Relative strength versus market and sector.
   The stock should resist market weakness, hold up better than peers, or sit inside a hot sector where relative strength is obvious.
8. `4 points` - Breakout is not extended.
   Entry should still be close enough to the pivot that the low-of-day stop keeps the risk tight, ideally within ADR or ATR.
9. `2 points` - Recognizable base structure.
   VCP, high-tight flag, Darvas box, tight channel, tight triangle, or flat range all count if the compression is real.
10. `1 point` - Excellent trigger-bar quality.
    A very narrow candle, inside bar, or unusually tight final day before breakout is a bonus, not the main driver.

### Optional ADR Overlay

- Minimum `ADR%` is not a published hard setup rule in the official Qullamaggie continuation-breakout material.
- Use ADR as a practical scan and execution overlay, not as doctrine.
- Preferred heuristic: `>6% ADR(20)`.
- Acceptable heuristic: `4.5%-6.0% ADR(20)`.
- Below roughly `4.5% ADR(20)`, down-rank unless the chart quality is exceptional.
- Also reject names already more than roughly `1 ADR` above the pivot or names whose entry-to-stop width is wider than about `1 ADR`.

### Inferred 5-Star Scan Overlays

These are not core structure rules. They are inferred from the repeated scan and ranking criteria used to surface the best continuation-breakout candidates before chart review.

- Top `1-2%` relative strength across the main `1-month`, `3-month`, and `6-month` lookback windows
- Up at least roughly `25%` in the past month, or otherwise clearly among the top recent movers surfaced by the leader scans
- Trading within roughly `2%` above or below the `10-day` SMA at scan time
- Current week volume at least `50%` lower than the prior week
- Within roughly `25%` of the `52-week` high
- In a leading sector or industry, not a lagging group
- Still fresh and near the pivot: `candidate` or `actionable`, not already `triggered` and `spent`
- Meets the liquidity floor: at least roughly `300,000` average daily shares and enough dollar volume for market-stop execution; elite names often clear stricter liquidity tiers
- No near-term earnings or obvious binary event unless the trade is intentionally being treated as an earnings setup

### 5-Star Hard Fails

If any of these are present, the setup should not be treated as `5-star`:

- No real prior momentum move
- Loose, wide, or obviously distributed base
- No higher lows into the pivot
- Price not respecting rising `10-day`, `20-day`, or `50-day` support
- No visible volume dry-up in the base
- Breakout already too extended from the pivot
- Weak stock in a weak sector
- Stop width that ruins the risk/reward profile

These are the repeated attributes highlighted in public example breakdowns:

- `W`: support on the `20-day`, higher lows, tight range, breakout candle, and extreme relative strength while the broader market was wobbling
- `PRTS`: big prior move, very clean trend, stayed above the `10-day`, then formed an exceptionally tight top range with drying volume
- `AMD`: smooth prior trend, perfect `20-day` support, then very tight action and volume dry-up before breakout
- `CELH`: big move, surfed the `10-day` and `20-day`, built higher lows off the `20-day`, then broke a tight high-tight-flag range

## Earnings And Episodic Pivot Rules

- The best earnings breakouts combine a real surprise with real structure: big beat, big growth, gap up, huge volume, and a breakout from a solid base.
- The best EP-style earnings names often break out of multi-month ranges and may have been building higher lows for months before the gap.
- Good earnings alone are not enough if the stock already had a major prior run and no fresh base. The market may have priced in the story already.
- Large gaps are not automatically too extended. A 30% earnings gap can still be the start of a much larger multi-month move if the revaluation is real.
- Huge volume is the key confirmation. Study both the chart and the actual earnings report, not just the headline.
- Multi-month range breakouts on earnings are among the most powerful entries in the playbook.
- These opportunities are infrequent. The edge comes from studying them deeply and being ready a few times a year, not from forcing constant trades.

## Mean Reversion, Bounce, And Parabolic-Long Notes

- Mean-reversion trades are fast-bounce trades. They should work quickly or be abandoned.
- Valid intraday triggers still use the same tight-entry logic: opening-range high, opening-range low, VWAP support, or VWAP failure.
- Gap-ups ruin mean-reversion longs and gap-downs ruin mean-reversion shorts because the extension has already partly corrected.
- A bounce play usually starts from a name that was previously very hot, then retraced roughly 50% or more, found moving-average support, and quickly started building higher lows.
- If the supposed bounce just stalls at the moving average for days, it is no longer a bounce setup.
- After a parabolic short unwind, a parabolic-long entry requires a very tight 60-minute range, higher lows, and a clean breakout with a tight stop. This is a specialized setup, not a default continuation trade.
- For very fast names with extremely high ADR, use smaller intraday timeframes because the daily 20-day moving average may be too slow to guide the entry.

## Parabolic Short Rules

- Treat parabolic shorts as an advanced setup. The long side offers enough opportunity without needing to force shorts.
- The setup usually starts after a stock has gone up massively for its size, becomes too fast and crowded, then shows the first real crack.
- Good entries come from first-red-day behavior, opening-range low breaks, VWAP failures, or lower highs into VWAP after an initial breakdown.
- Tight intraday ranges that break down after the first crack can be used for adds, but only after structure develops.
- Backside context matters: moving averages that acted as support on the way up often become resistance on the way down.
- Targets are usually the 10-day or 20-day moving average, or a clear support area roughly 40-50% down from the peak.
- Prefer liquid former leaders and megacaps on the short side. Avoid low-priced small caps where borrow, spreads, and squeeze risk distort the trade.
- Use the weekly or monthly chart to judge just how stretched the move really is before deciding whether the name is truly parabolic.

For the dedicated short reference, use [Qullamaggie Parabolic Short](./qullamaggie-parabolic-short.md).

## Risk And Trade Management

- Standard account risk is usually 0.3-0.5% per trade and only rarely above 1%.
- Minimum daily dollar volume should be at least roughly `position_size * 200` so execution quality stays acceptable.
- Stops should normally be tighter than 1 ADR or 1 ATR from entry, and many of the best entries are materially tighter than that.
- Initial long stop usually goes under the low of day. More aggressive entries can use the opening-range low.
- Never widen stops. Taking the small loss is the whole model.
- Re-entry is allowed, but only on a fresh setup such as renewed higher lows off the 10-day or 20-day after the first stop-out.
- If the setup does not go quickly enough, lighten or exit. Sideways action near entry for 3-5 days is a real warning sign.
- Use the 1-minute and 5-minute charts only around the open. Later in the day, the swing thesis should come from the daily or 60-minute structure, not micro noise.
- Avoid fresh long entries within roughly 3 trading days of earnings unless the trade is explicitly an earnings setup or already has a meaningful profit cushion.
- Missed entries stay missed. Do not chase because of FOMO.

## Profit Taking And Trailing Rules

- The repeatable bull-market template is trim and trail.
- Common long-side management is to sell roughly 33-50% after day 3-5 if the trade is working, then move the stop on the remainder to breakeven.
- Trail the runner under the 10-day moving average for faster names or under the 20-day for slower names.
- For many standard longs, a daily close below the chosen moving average is the exit signal.
- On highly overextended or parabolic names, switch to faster protection such as the 10 EMA or 20 EMA on the 60-minute chart.
- If a stock goes straight up 200-500% in a short period, traditional daily moving-average exits are too loose and will give back too much.
- The 10-day and 20-day moving averages are both breakout support references on the way up and mean-reversion magnets on the way down.

## Portfolio Management

- Own the fastest names in strong bull phases. Slow movers tie up buying power that should be in leaders.
- Cull anything not acting right, long or short. Capital should keep rotating toward the best performers.
- Add aggression only when existing positions are already deep in the green. Fresh risk is most dangerous when the trade is newly opened.
- Reduce long exposure when euphoria becomes obvious, even if the indexes still look healthy.
- Public return-posting and obvious social-media euphoria are practical caution signals, even if the index charts still look fine.
- Review open positions continuously. Anything that stops acting well while the market weakens should be the first candidate for reduction.
- Most intraday noise does not matter for swing decisions. The key exposure decisions happen into the close, especially 5-10 minutes before the bell.
- After a very strong month, step back and get calmer. Aggressive conditions are often followed by digestion.

## Scans And Watchlist Rules

- If a scan returns more than roughly 50 names, tighten the filters. Increase the rank threshold, ADR filter, or both.
- No sub-dollar names belong in this framework.
- Keep separate lists for the hottest sectors and the best symbols inside each sector. Ranking by liquidity helps decide what is actually tradable.
- A practical leader scan family is the 1-month, 3-month, and 6-month gainers list, with ADR and daily-volume filters layered on top.
- Historical formulas from the research memo:
  - 1-month gainers: `100 * (C / C21 - 1)`
  - 3-month gainers: `100 * (C / C63 - 1)`
  - 6-month gainers: `100 * (C / C126 - 1)`
- Also study the biggest 2-year winners and the leaders from prior hot themes. This is part of building intuition for what real outsized winners looked like before they were obvious.
- Alerting and tracking matter. If you want big gains, keep a living watchlist of what is hot and review it regularly.

## Research Process And Psychology

- FOMO, chasing, and stop-widening are account killers. Most traders fail because they cannot accept the small loss.
- A low win rate is acceptable if the losses stay small and the winners are allowed to reach 10R, 20R, or more.
- Avoid scalping. This framework is built around asymmetric swing outcomes, not constant small intraday churn.
- Defense comes first. Be passionate about stops, not about stocks.
- Listening to the market means watching actual price behavior, not forcing a macro opinion onto charts.
- The real work starts after the close. Review charts, study prior leaders, track hot sectors, and keep building pattern memory.
- The grind matters. The edge comes from thousands of hours of study, repetition, and post-market work most people do not do.
