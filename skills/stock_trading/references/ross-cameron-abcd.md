# Ross Cameron ABCD Pattern

Classic momentum continuation pattern. The stock makes an impulse move, pulls back in an orderly way, and then resumes through the prior pivot.

## Basic Structure

- **A to B**: initial impulse higher
- **B to C**: controlled pullback
- **C to D**: continuation move higher

Ross Cameron uses this as another way to frame the same core momentum idea: buy the continuation after an orderly pause.

## Scanning For Candidates

This setup usually comes from the same morning momentum universe rather than a separate scanner.

Look for:

- A stock already meeting the 5 Pillars
- A strong initial impulse leg
- A controlled B-to-C pullback
- Volume contraction into the pullback
- A clear continuation pivot for the D leg

Exclude immediately:

- Random stocks that are not already in play
- Deep, messy pullbacks
- Overlapping candle structures with no clear A-B-C-D sequence
- Late-day low-volume versions where the pattern is more label than edge

## Pattern Requirements

- The initial move must be strong and obvious
- The pullback should be controlled, not a collapse
- Volume should usually contract during the pullback
- The continuation trigger should be clear
- The stock should still satisfy the broader catalyst, volume, and float filters

## What A Good Setup Looks Like

- The A-to-B leg is clean and impulsive
- The B-to-C retracement is orderly and not too deep
- The pivot is obvious enough that other traders can see it too
- The stock is still the key momentum name on the screen
- Price expands cleanly once D triggers

## What A Weak Setup Looks Like

- The initial leg is mediocre
- The pullback is too deep or too long
- Volume does not contract during the pause
- The pivot level is fuzzy or debatable
- The stock is just drifting, not actually under momentum pressure

## Trap Setups And Red Herrings

- **Label-everything-ABCD trap:** forcing an ABCD label onto any random zig-zag
- **Too-deep-C trap:** the retracement destroys the continuation structure but the trader still tries to force the pattern
- **Already-extended-D trap:** buying after the move has already triggered and extended
- **Non-leader trap:** the pattern exists on paper, but the stock is not actually the leading momentum name anymore

## Entry

- Buy as price breaks the relevant continuation pivot, often effectively the prior intraday high / point B area
- On very strong names he may use a starter before the exact break, then add on confirmation

## Stop Loss

- Stop usually goes below point C or the low of the pullback

## Management Rules

- Demand at least 2:1 reward to risk before entry
- Scale out as the continuation confirms
- If the move fails to extend after the trigger, exit quickly

## Best Use

- Strong small-cap momentum stocks already in play
- Morning session when continuation patterns are still resolving cleanly

## Relationship To Other Setups

- Overlaps heavily with the bull flag / first pullback logic
- The difference is mostly descriptive: ABCD emphasizes the impulse-pullback-continuation sequence, while bull flag emphasizes the flag structure near highs

## No-Trade Conditions

- The pullback is disorderly or too deep
- The stock is no longer the leading momentum name
- The pivot trigger is ambiguous
