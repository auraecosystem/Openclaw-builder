# Canadian Tax Optimization Strategies

## RRSP Timing

Contribute in **high-income years** (marginal rate 40%+), withdraw in **low-income years** (retirement, sabbatical, parental leave, between jobs). The spread between contribution and withdrawal tax rates is your profit.

## Income Splitting

- **Spousal RRSP:** Higher earner contributes, gets deduction. Lower-earning spouse withdraws in retirement at their lower rate. **3-year attribution rule:** if spouse withdraws within 3 calendar years of contribution, income is attributed back to contributor.
- **Pension income splitting (65+):** Split up to 50% of eligible pension income with spouse. Huge savings if one spouse has pension and the other doesn't.
- **TFSA gifting:** Higher earner gives lower earner money to contribute to TFSA. **No attribution rules** apply to TFSAs — all growth belongs to the account holder. Legal and effective.

## Capital Gains Strategies

- **Capital gains inclusion:** 50% of gains are taxable (rate unchanged for 2025-2026).
- **Tax-loss harvesting:** Sell losing investments to realize losses that offset gains. Can carry losses back 3 years or forward indefinitely. Watch the "superficial loss" rule: can't repurchase same security within 30 days.
- **Timing gains:** Defer selling profitable investments until a low-income year. Or realize gains annually up to the bottom of your current tax bracket.

## TFSA Optimization

- **December withdrawal trick:** Withdraw in December. On January 1, your contribution room increases by the full withdrawal amount plus new annual room ($7,000). If $5K grew to $15K, withdrawing $15K gives you $15K + $7K = $22K room on Jan 1.
- **Prioritize growth assets in TFSA:** The more growth potential, the more tax-free benefit. Index ETFs > GICs in TFSA.

## FHSA Arbitrage

Open an FHSA even if you're unsure about buying. Contributions are tax-deductible immediately. If you never buy, transfer to RRSP without using RRSP room (deadline: 15 years after opening or age 71). Essentially free RRSP room.

## Ontario Marginal Tax Rates (2025, approximate)

| Taxable Income | Combined Marginal Rate |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Under $55,867 | ~20% |
| $55,867 - $100,392 | ~30% |
| $100,392 - $150,000 | ~43% |
| $150,000 - $220,000 | ~46% |
| Over $220,000 | ~53% |

**Key insight:** RRSP contributions are most valuable when your marginal rate is 30%+. Below that, TFSA is usually better.

## Quick Wins

- Claim **all** medical expenses (aggregate across 12-month period, not just calendar year)
- Don't forget **moving expenses** if you relocated 40+ km for work
- Claim **interest on student loans** (federal + provincial)
- **Charitable donation bundling:** Combine multiple years of donations into one year to exceed the $200 threshold where the credit rate jumps from 15% to 29%
