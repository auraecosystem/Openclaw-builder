# Cross-disciplinary methods for precursor detection and event modelling in large ensembles of nonstationary multivariate time series

## Problem formalization and diagnostic invariants

Your setting aligns with several mature problem formulations across statistics, signal processing, dynamical systems, and modern ML:

- **Multivariate time-series anomaly detection (TSAD)**, where anomalies may be point, contextual, or collective/subsequence patterns rather than isolated outliers.
- **Change-point / regime-shift detection**, where the generative dynamics switch abruptly or gradually (including changes in dependence structure, variance, spectrum, or latent state).
- **Critical-transition early warning**, where one seeks precursors to sudden state changes in non-autonomous real-world systems (often with strong nonstationarity and imperfect observability).

Your target archetype is structurally specific: an interval of reduced local dispersion (in primary measurements plus a correlated-but-rescaled activity proxy) followed by a rapid, persistent, monotonic excursion. This combines (i) volatility/dispersion contraction signatures, (ii) cross-signal coupling (primary vs proxy), and (iii) monotone-run/excursion structure with well-defined onset/termination conditions.

Your stated data characteristics (nonstationary, heteroskedastic, fat-tailed, structural phase transitions; long horizons; large panels) are repeatedly highlighted as core failure modes for "standard" TSAD pipelines and benchmarks, motivating robust evaluation and model classes that explicitly accommodate drift, regime changes, and heavy tails.

## Cross-disciplinary analogues of the compression-to-excursion archetype

The "quiet period -> transition" motif appears in many domains, but the direction of precursor signals (variance up vs down, entropy up vs down) and the dominant observable vary widely -- an important limitation if transferring methods.

| Analogue domain | What is "the event" | How precursors are operationalized | Noted limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complex systems / tipping points | Regime shift / critical transition | Generic early-warning indicators; ML trained on surrogates and non-equilibrium indicators; warning works even without classic critical slowing down. | Many EWS families implicitly assume a particular transition mechanism (e.g., local bifurcation) and can degrade in "non-autonomous" settings or with measurement/recording artifacts. |
| Socio-technical / online systems | Abrupt coordination/structure change | Interpretable ML early warnings for online transitions (coordination/innovation/complexity shifts preceding transitions). | Transferability across contexts is an explicit focus; features may not generalize if the mechanism differs. |
| Seismology / geophysics | Earthquake-related anomalies / precursors | Multi-parameter precursor detection and ML-assisted seismology workflows; often framed as anomaly detection in correlated geophysical channels. | Label scarcity, imbalance, and nonrepeatability of large events complicate validation; many claimed precursors are unstable across regions/time. |
| Materials / structural failure | Imminent failure / damage stage transition | Acoustic emission (AE) and other sensing time series analyzed for multistage precursors (decomposition-based and critical-fluctuation-style methods). | Strong dependence on sensor placement, loading protocol, and heterogeneity; heavy-tailed bursts and nonstationary noise are common. |
| Neuroscience / physiology | Seizure onset or other transition | Reviews emphasize multistage pipelines: feature engineering + ML/DL; improved learning paradigms for time-series prediction tasks including seizure prediction. | Cross-subject generalization and real-time constraints are persistent failure points; "pre-ictal" states can be nonstationary and patient-specific. |
| Epidemiology / public health surveillance | Outbreak onset across multiple sites | Multisite outbreak detection uses generalized linear modelling, scan statistics, Bayesian spatiotemporal models, and explicit alerting thresholds. | Reporting delays, heteroskedastic counts, and shifting baselines (policy/testing) can mimic outbreaks; multiple-testing control matters. |

**Two broad transferable takeaways:**

1. Precursor signatures are often "multi-view": using additional proxies/channels improves detectability but introduces scale-mismatch, dependence shifts, and missingness issues.
2. Evaluation and overfitting control dominate outcomes when events are rare, regimes shift, and benchmarks are noisy -- so modern research increasingly builds benchmarking discipline into the method story.

## Method families for precursor detection and ensemble-relative scoring

| Method / framework family | Origin domain(s) | Structural property addressed | Known limitations / failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Robust rolling dispersion / contraction indices (MAD/IQR, robust CV, multivariate dispersion monitoring) | Statistical process control; robust statistics | Directly targets low-dispersion precursor via windowed dispersion and its contraction; multivariate variants can incorporate primary+proxy jointly. | Window-length sensitivity; heavy tails and intermittent bursts can dominate dispersion estimates unless robustified; nonstationary baselines can create false "contractions" after regime changes. |
| Multi-scale time-frequency decomposition features (wavelets, Fourier+wavelet hybrids, EMD) | Signal processing; geophysics; structural health | Captures scale-separated signatures and nonstationary spectral changes; EMD and wavelets are widely used for multi-stage precursors. | Decomposition artefacts under abrupt change points; sensitivity to irregular sampling and boundary effects; multiple-resolution features enlarge the tuning space and can increase overfitting. |
| Change-point detection (CPD) as precursor logic (variance/mean/slope/spectrum/dependence CPD; offline and online) | Statistics; econometrics; signal processing | Targets structural phase transitions and can formalize onset of dispersion contraction or onset of excursion as distinct CPD types. | Many CPD methods are type-specific (mean vs variance vs dependence) and miss "wrong" changes; dependence across channels/series complicates null calibration; heavy tails can inflate false positives without robust CPD. |
| Bayesian online change-point detection (BOCPD) and modern modular variants | Bayesian statistics | Provides explicit run-length posterior for online detection and naturally yields a score for "we are in a long stable regime" vs "a change is likely." | Requires modelling choices (hazard function, likelihood family) that can be brittle under heavy tails, nonstationary variance, or misspecified observation models; computational scaling across 10k series depends on amortization/approximation. |
| Collective / subsequence anomaly detection (joint models of collective anomalies + change points) | Data mining; industrial monitoring | Your behaviour archetype is naturally a collective anomaly (pattern over an interval), not a single-point outlier; joint collective-anomaly + CP models explicitly target this. | Defining "collective" boundaries can be ambiguous under drift; many benchmarks over-emphasize point anomalies -- methods may look good yet fail on long-horizon, regime-switching realities. |
| Matrix Profile (MP) and motif/discord primitives (streaming and offline) | Time-series data mining | MP is a scalable, largely parameter-light primitive for motif discovery and subsequence anomaly (discord) detection; suitable for large archives and streams. | Requires a subsequence length; distance choices can be distorted by heteroskedasticity and drift unless normalized carefully; multivariate MP variants add design choices for channel weighting. |
| Motif discovery under time warping + MDL-style scoring (DTW motifs, MDL ranking) | Pattern mining; information theory | Captures "same pattern at different rates," relevant when precursor/excursion timing varies; MDL scoring is a principled complexity penalty for pattern selection. | DTW can be computationally heavy at scale; MDL choices can be sensitive to model class; time warping can also align unrelated dynamics (spurious matches) in fat-tailed data. |
| Shapelets and fast convolutional feature transforms (ROCKET-family; shapelet-transform variants; shapelet+Transformers) | Time-series classification; interpretable ML | Turn local shapes (including "flat then surge") into features that can be used for ranking; modern variants are extremely fast and can scale to many series. | Primarily developed for labelled classification; for unsupervised precursor ranking you still need a reference/normal model; feature sets can be brittle under strong nonstationarity unless drift-aware recalibration is built in. |
| Deep TSAD via Transformers (attention discrepancy, reconstruction/prediction, variable-temporal attention) | Deep learning; industrial monitoring | Learns multivariate temporal+cross-variable dependency and yields anomaly/novelty scores; transformer variants explicitly model variable correlation and long-range patterns. | Documented issues include overfitting to benchmark artefacts and inconsistent evaluation; some reconstruction methods can "fit" anomalies; may struggle when distribution shifts rapidly. |
| Deep TSAD with diffusion models (coarse-to-fine, physically-guided, transformer-diffusion hybrids) | Generative modelling; anomaly detection | Trains a generative model of "normal" and uses denoising difficulty / likelihood surrogates for anomaly scores; multi-resolution diffusion explicitly targets nonstationarity. | Training cost and calibration complexity; diffusion likelihoods are subtle and may not map cleanly to ranking scores; domain shift can destroy generative assumptions without adaptation. |
| Self-supervised representation learning (contrastive, masked, seasonal-trend disentangling) used for scoring | Representation learning; causality-inspired TS modelling | Produces embeddings for timestamps/subsequences so you can score relative similarity to normal histories and build cross-sectional ranks from distances. | Learned invariances depend on augmentations/masking; embeddings can drift over decades of data and require continual recalibration; may under-represent rare phase-transition behaviours. |
| Long-context sequence backbones (patch-based Transformers; selective state-space models) as building blocks | ML for long sequences | Addresses long histories by architectural design: patching reduces attention cost; selective SSMs target linear-time scaling. | Generally developed for forecasting/sequence modelling; converting them into robust anomaly/precursor rankers still needs calibration, drift handling, and evaluation discipline. |
| Time-series foundation models (pretrained tokenization/quantization or MoE designs) adapted for TSAD-style scoring | Foundation models for time series | Zero-shot / few-shot modelling can provide expected-trajectory baselines and uncertainty proxies; MoE and tokenization approaches explicitly aim to cover heterogeneous series families. | Foundation models are mostly benchmarked for forecasting; anomaly/precursor scoring requires careful post-hoc calibration; some studies note that "simpler" methods can outperform advanced models on curated TSAD benchmarks. |
| Graph neural networks for time series (GNN4TS; spatiotemporal graphs; graph-based anomaly scoring) | Spatiotemporal modelling; network science | Explicitly models inter-variable and/or inter-series structure -- useful when ensemble-relative scoring depends on population coupling or shared drivers. | Graph structure is often unknown and learned -- risking instability under drift; scaling to 10k nodes can be expensive; dependence shifts can trigger false alerts unless graph is time-varying and robust. |
| Visibility graphs and network encodings of time series (including trend-sensitive visibility variants) | Complexity science; nonlinear time series | Converts trajectories into graphs so regime changes appear as changes in topology; some methods explicitly distinguish rising vs falling trend structures. | Graph encodings can be sensitive to preprocessing (detrending/differencing); interpretability depends on stable mapping from dynamics to topology; heavy tails can dominate graph statistics. |
| Topological data analysis (persistent homology; persistent entropy; topology-based anomaly/CPD) | Topology + statistics; complex systems | Targets structural changes (including dependence structure) not easily reduced to mean/variance shifts; has been used for early warning and CPD in financial and other systems. | Computation and parameterization (embedding dimension, filtration choices); sensitivity to sampling density and noise; can be overkill if simpler invariants separate the archetype. |
| Entropy/complexity indicators (permutation entropy; non-equilibrium EWS; permutation-pattern precursors) | Information theory; physics; combustion | Captures precursor behaviour as changes in complexity/ordering rather than amplitude; used for early detection of tipping points and instability diagnostics. | Entropy measures can respond to sampling rate and preprocessing; "direction" of change (up/down) depends on system; nonstationary trends can masquerade as entropy shifts. |
| Recurrence plots / recurrence quantification analysis (RQA) and recurrence networks | Nonlinear dynamics | Detects transitions via changes in recurrence structure; applied to complex systems including financial markets and other nonstationary series. | Requires embedding choices; can be unstable for short windows; heavy tails and measurement noise can substantially alter recurrence structure. |
| Conformal prediction and conformal anomaly detection (p-values as scores; adaptive/weighted conformal for time series) | Statistical learning theory | Produces calibrated, interpretable scores (often p-values) tied to false-alarm rates; recent work addresses temporal dependence and distribution shift and also targets anomaly detection. | Conformal calibration is stressed by change points and nonstationarity; specialized variants are proposed for time series with change points and for online adaptation. |
| Scan statistics / multisite outbreak-style detection for ranking streams | Spatial statistics; epidemiology; streaming detection | Provides ensemble-relative scores by scanning windows and comparing local statistics to baseline across sites/streams; explicit control of false alarms is central. | Assumptions about baseline stationarity and reporting can be violated; multiple-testing across many streams needs careful handling; surges due to system-wide drift can bias ranks. |
| Concept drift detection + adaptation (unsupervised drift alarms; meta-learning for drift) used as a wrapper around scoring | Data stream mining; online learning | Detects when the "normal" distribution changes, enabling rebaselining of precursor scores and reducing false positives under long-term drift. | Drift detectors can confuse rare true events with drift; adaptation can forget recurrence patterns; coupling drift alarms to event alarms requires careful objective design. |

**A recurring, evidence-backed theme**: benchmark results can invert once data quality, evaluation metrics, and tuning discipline are tightened -- sometimes favouring simpler statistical baselines over complex neural architectures.

## Event boundary estimation and excursion modelling

Once a series is flagged as a candidate, "boundary estimation" (onset/termination) becomes a segmentation and stopping-time problem.

| Method / framework family | Origin domain(s) | Structural property addressed | Known limitations / failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offline CPD / segmentation for onset and termination (including deep-generated CPD) | Statistics; ML | After-the-fact onset/termination can be posed as finding change points in variance, slope, or dependence; recent work trains neural nets to generate CPD procedures automatically. | If the excursion is smooth rather than abrupt, CPD may place boundaries inconsistently; heavy tails can create false boundaries; multivariate dependence changes may dominate amplitude changes. |
| Switching state-space / regime-switching models (HMM/SLDS variants) | Control/statistics; neuroscience; econometrics | Formalizes segmentation as inference over latent regimes (quiet vs excursion vs post-event), yielding probabilistic state boundaries. | Requires choosing regime count and observation model; misspecification under fat tails and nonstationary variance can cause state fragmentation or "sticky" regimes. |
| Trend filtering / piecewise-linear (L1) trend methods (and learned/dynamic variants) | Convex optimisation; spatiotemporal geoscience | Monotone or piecewise-linear excursions can be fit and boundaries inferred from "kinks" or trend points; recent work explores adaptive/dynamic trend filtering. | Tail events can be treated as kinks/noise depending on regularization; strong heteroskedasticity can bias trend points unless loss is robust or weighted. |
| First-passage / hitting-time modelling for threshold-based termination | Statistical physics; stochastic processes | Your termination rules ("positive/negative threshold exceedance relative to onset") are naturally first-passage events; recent work refines threshold-crossing probability and detection-time modelling. | First-passage models often assume simplified dynamics; empirical nonstationarity and structural breaks can violate assumptions, requiring time-varying or data-driven hazard rates. |
| Discrete-time survival / hazard models (including deep survival) for time-to-termination | Biostatistics; ML | Turns termination into a probabilistic time-to-event problem; discrete-time survival explicitly matches discrete sampling and supports covariate trajectories. | Censoring/label definition is hard if "event end" is operational; hazard models can be miscalibrated under drift unless re-estimated or conformalized. |
| Boundary-aware tokenization / boundary-aware Transformers (from sequential boundary detection literature) | Sequence modelling; event segmentation | Recent work argues fixed tokenization can obscure regime changes and proposes boundary-aware tokenization; online generic boundary detection is formalized in sequential perception tasks. | Often developed for video/action streams; transferring to numeric TS requires defining "eventness" and handling long-range drift and heteroskedasticity. |
| Sequential tests for stopping rules (CUSUM/EWMA variants, robust SPC charts, quickest-change in AR) | Quality control; information theory | Online termination (or onset) can be formalized as a quickest-change/stopping problem; modern work extends quickest-change to AR processes and robust/nonparametric control charts. | Classical optimality often assumes known pre/post distributions; robustness to model mismatch and heavy tails is an active research area; multivariate dependence shifts complicate control limits. |

## Parameter optimisation and anti-overfitting guardrails

Your parameter-optimisation subproblem ("10-20 continuous parameters; maximize a composite historical objective while penalizing sample-specific exploitation") matches a broad set of optimization + validation toolchains.

| Method / framework family | Origin domain(s) | Structural property addressed | Known limitations / failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benchmark-driven tuning discipline (large curated benchmarks + unified tuning) | TSAD evaluation research | Tight benchmark design can expose overfitting and ranking instability; prominent recent efforts emphasize dataset integrity, metric reliability, and thorough tuning across many algorithms. | Benchmarks may still not match your phenomenon (compression to excursion); metrics can reward the "wrong" boundary behaviour; curated datasets can bias toward certain anomaly types. |
| Hyperparameter optimization frameworks for TSAD (meta-models of validation error; learned parameterization) | AutoML; database systems | Explicitly built to tune anomaly detection parameters under limited labels, learning parameterization models for TSAD algorithms. | If the validation protocol leaks future information or is not regime-aware, HPO can still exploit path-specific structure; tuning objectives can mismatch real costs (false alarms vs missed events). |
| Bayesian optimization with blocked / time-series-aware cross-validation | Bayesian optimization; applied forecasting | Uses chronology-respecting CV folds; recent work explores blocked time series CV inside Bayesian optimization loops. | Fold design is decisive under long memory and structural breaks; blocked CV can still be optimistic if regimes recur or overlap in feature space; computational cost can be high for 10k-series panels. |
| Evolutionary search / neural architecture search tuned for time series anomaly detection | Evolutionary computation; TSAD | Searches for minimal/optimal architectures for TS anomaly detection, reducing manual design. | Search can overfit to benchmark artefacts; compute-heavy; may discover architectures that exploit nuisance structure unless validated across regimes/series families. |
| Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) as a penalty against sample-specific structure | Optimization theory; operations research | DRO optimizes worst-case performance over an ambiguity set; recent work includes time-decayed / Wasserstein-style DRO for nonstationarity. | Choosing the ambiguity set is an art; worst-case objectives can be conservative; converting event-detection objectives into DRO form requires careful surrogate losses. |
| Drift-aware wrappers around scoring (unsupervised drift detection; recurring drift mitigation; meta-learning) | Data streams; continual learning | Provides a mechanism to adapt parameters or calibrations when the "normal" distribution changes, reducing path-dependence over decades of data. | Drift alarms can fire on true rare events (confounding detection vs adaptation); continual adaptation risks catastrophic forgetting when old regimes recur. |
| MDL / description-length penalties in pattern discovery | Information theory; motif discovery | MDL-style objectives explicitly penalize overly complex explanations and can be integrated into motif/collective anomaly selection. | MDL depends on the coding/model class; may underfit subtle multi-channel precursors; can be computationally heavy when searching many candidate motifs. |
| Post-hoc calibration for false-alarm control (conformal methods applied to TSAD) | Conformal inference | Converts arbitrary scores into calibrated p-values / interpretable false-alarm rates; recent work explicitly targets anomaly detection under temporal dependence and leverages pretrained models. | Conformal validity is strained by change points and distribution shifts; methods proposing change-point-aware conformal variants highlight this as an open issue. |

## Cross-cutting limitations and failure modes

Several failure modes recur across essentially all families above:

**Nonstationarity and structural phase transitions break "fixed normal" assumptions.** Many TSAD systems implicitly learn a stationary normal manifold; in long-horizon panels with regime shifts this creates false positives/negatives unless drift detection, regime modelling, or continual recalibration is incorporated.

**Heteroskedasticity and fat tails distort both dispersion-based precursors and learned reconstruction errors.** Classical least-squares style estimators are particularly sensitive, prompting robust CPD tests (M-estimation / LAD) and heavy-tail-aware filters (e.g., Student-t / Pearson-type models in state-space filtering).

**Ensemble-relative scoring can fail under population-wide drift.** If many (or all) series drift together, ranks/percentiles can remain stable while absolute risk increases; conversely, if a subset shifts due to a shared exogenous driver, ranks may spuriously amplify group effects. This is one reason scan-statistic and spatiotemporal surveillance literature emphasizes explicit baselines and probabilistic modelling of background vs outbreak states.

**Benchmarking pathologies and metric choice can dominate apparent progress.** Recent TSAD benchmarking work highlights flawed datasets, biased evaluation measures, and inconsistent practices; under more careful curation and tuning, simpler methods can outperform complex ones, and the ranking of methods can flip.

**Computational scaling constraints shape the feasible hypothesis space.** For 100-10,000 parallel series with years-to-decades history, practical approaches often rely on primitives that scale linearly/near-linearly (e.g., selective state-space sequence models, scalable Matrix Profile variants, patching designs in Transformers) or on amortized/shared models across series.
